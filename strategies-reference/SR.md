# Identifying Support & Resistance on a 1Y / 1D Chart

A 1-year chart with daily (1D) candles shows ~252 trading days. Each candle = one day. This timeframe is ideal for spotting **major, reliable** support/resistance levels because noise is filtered out and only significant turning points stand out.

---

## 1. Understand the Concepts

- **Support** — a price level where buying pressure repeatedly stops a decline. Price "bounces" up from it. Acts as a floor.
- **Resistance** — a price level where selling pressure repeatedly stops an advance. Price "rejects" down from it. Acts as a ceiling.
- **Role reversal** — once broken, support often becomes resistance, and vice versa. This is one of the most important confirmations.

---

## 2. Step-by-Step Method

### Step 1 — Zoom out and look for clusters
Scan the full year. Identify horizontal price zones the chart **visited multiple times**. Don't look for exact prices — look for **zones** (a small band, e.g., $48–$50), since real markets rarely turn at the exact same tick.

### Step 2 — Count the touches
- **2 touches** = a possible level.
- **3+ touches** = a confirmed, strong level.
The more times price reacted at a zone, the more significant it is.

### Step 3 — Mark swing highs and swing lows
- **Swing high** = a peak with lower candles on both sides → potential resistance.
- **Swing low** = a trough with higher candles on both sides → potential support.
On a 1D chart, focus on the prominent swings, not every tiny wiggle.

### Step 4 — Draw horizontal lines
Draw a horizontal line through each cluster of swing points. Connect the **bodies** for general zones, or the **wicks/highs-lows** for extreme levels. Many traders shade a rectangle to represent the zone rather than a single line.

### Step 5 — Note the strongest levels
Rank levels by:
- Number of touches
- How sharply price reacted (strong rejection = stronger level)
- How recent the touches are (recent = more relevant)
- Volume at the level (high volume = more meaningful)

---

## 3. Confirming Signals

| Signal | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| Long wicks rejecting a level | Strong buying/selling defending the zone |
| Multiple closes failing to break | Level is respected |
| Volume spike at the level | Institutional interest, level is real |
| Role reversal after a break | Confirms the level's validity |
| Round numbers (e.g., 100, 50) | Often act as psychological S/R |

---

## 4. Helpful Tools (Optional)

- **Moving averages** — the 50-day and 200-day MAs often act as dynamic support/resistance on a 1D chart.
- **Trendlines** — connect rising lows (uptrend support) or falling highs (downtrend resistance) for diagonal S/R.
- **Volume profile** — shows price levels with the most traded volume (high-volume nodes = strong S/R).
- **Fibonacci retracement** — drawn from the year's major low to high, the 38.2%, 50%, and 61.8% levels often align with S/R.

---

## 5. Quick Checklist

- [ ] Zoomed out to the full 1Y view
- [ ] Identified zones touched 2+ times (prefer 3+)
- [ ] Marked major swing highs (resistance) and swing lows (support)
- [ ] Drew zones as rectangles, not just thin lines
- [ ] Checked for role reversals after breaks
- [ ] Confirmed with volume and candle rejection
- [ ] Noted the 50-day and 200-day MAs

---

## 6. Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Treating levels as exact prices** — they're zones.
2. **Drawing too many lines** — keep only the levels that clearly matter.
3. **Ignoring volume** — a level with no volume behind it is weak.
4. **Forcing levels** — if a zone wasn't respected, don't keep it.
5. **Forgetting context** — S/R behaves differently in a trend vs. a range.

---

*Support and resistance is an art as much as a science. Levels indicate **probability**, not certainty — always combine with risk management.*
